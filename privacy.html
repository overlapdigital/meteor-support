Meteor Privacy Policy

Last updated: 5 November 2026

1. Introduction

Meteor is a macOS application that helps you stay on top of meetings with reminders, countdowns and optional notification banners.

This policy explains what Meteor accesses, what it stores and the choices available to you. References to “we”, “us” and “our” mean Thomas Williams, an individual developer based in the United Kingdom. Thomas Williams is the controller of personal information received through support and privacy enquiries.

Meteor processes calendar information locally on your Mac. It does not require a Meteor account, upload your calendar information to the developer or use third-party advertising or analytics services.

2. Calendar information

With your permission, Meteor reads calendars available through macOS using Apple’s EventKit framework. This can include:

  • Calendar names, identifiers and calendar source names.
  • Meeting titles, start and end times, locations and event identifiers.
  • Meeting links in an event’s URL, location or notes.
  • Event status and your attendance response, so cancelled or declined meetings can be excluded.

Meteor uses this information to display your agenda, schedule reminders and countdowns, and provide access to supported meeting links. Event notes are examined locally to find meeting links.

Meteor reads calendar events; it does not create, edit or delete them.

3. Calendar accounts and meeting services

Meteor reads calendars already available on your Mac. It does not sign in directly to Google Calendar, Microsoft 365 or other calendar services, and it does not ask for your calendar account password.

If you use a calendar account that synchronises with a cloud service, that synchronisation is managed separately by macOS and your calendar provider, under their own privacy arrangements.

If you choose to join a meeting, Meteor opens its link in your browser or meeting application. The browser, meeting application and meeting provider then handle information under their own privacy policies.

4. Optional name and preferences

You can optionally enter a first name or nickname to personalise reminders. You can skip this step, and change or clear the name in Meteor’s Settings.

Your chosen name is stored locally and is not sent to the developer. Meteor does not obtain it from your system account or contacts.

Other locally saved preferences include selected calendars, reminder timing, sound, display coverage, notification choices, and pause and Focus Filter settings.

5. Local storage

Meteor keeps calendar details in memory while running. It also saves limited local records so it can remember snoozed, dismissed or otherwise handled reminders across app restarts. These records contain calendar and event identifiers, timing information and reminder status; they do not contain meeting titles, notes or meeting links.

When configured, Meteor’s Focus Filter integration stores a local history of the reminder rules applied and when they changed. This allows reminders due during a pause to remain suppressed after a restart.

Meteor does not operate a central calendar database or provide its own cloud synchronisation. Local information may also be included in backups you choose to make of your Mac; those backups are managed separately from Meteor.

6. Notifications and reminders

Meteor displays reminders within the application. If you enable optional notification banners, it also passes the meeting title, reminder message and scheduled delivery time to macOS Notification Center.

Meeting titles may therefore appear in banners or Notification Center, depending on your macOS notification settings. Meteor does not use a remote push-notification service to deliver these reminders.

You can manage Meteor’s notification permission in System Settings → Notifications, and turn optional banners on or off in Meteor’s Settings.

7. Analytics and sharing

Meteor does not include third-party advertising, analytics, usage-tracking or crash-reporting SDKs. It does not sell calendar information or share it with advertisers or data brokers.

Calendar information is not transmitted to the developer. Information you choose to send in a support or privacy enquiry is handled separately as described below.

8. Retention and deletion

Local preferences, including your optional name, remain until you change or remove them or delete the relevant application data. Pause and Focus Filter history also remains locally until the relevant application data is removed.

Reminder records expire 24 hours after the meeting’s recorded end time. Meteor removes expired records when it next evaluates reminders while running; records may remain on disk while the app is closed.

Meteor withdraws pending notification banners when it quits normally. Banners already delivered may remain in Notification Center until cleared by you or macOS.

You can revoke calendar access through System Settings → Privacy & Security → Calendars. This stops Meteor reading your calendars and providing calendar-based reminders. Revoking permission does not automatically delete previously saved local settings or reminder records.

You can uninstall Meteor to stop using it. Removing the application does not necessarily remove its preferences, shared Focus Filter data, delivered notifications or copies in backups. Those may need to be removed separately. Your original calendar events are managed by macOS and your calendar provider.

9. Support and privacy enquiries

If you contact us, we receive your email address and any information you choose to include. We use this information to respond, provide support and handle related follow-up. You do not need to send calendar contents or meeting links to make an enquiry.

For ordinary support correspondence, our lawful basis under the UK GDPR is our legitimate interest in answering enquiries and supporting Meteor. Where processing is necessary to meet a legal obligation, such as responding to an applicable data protection rights request, we rely on that obligation.

Support correspondence is kept while the enquiry is open and for as long as reasonably necessary to address related follow-up or resolve an outstanding dispute. Information that is no longer needed is deleted.

We use Google Gmail to receive, store and reply to support emails. Google processes the messages and associated information to provide its email service, and also handles information under its own Privacy Policy. Please avoid sending sensitive information, passwords or private meeting links. Providing information for support is voluntary, but we may be unable to resolve an enquiry without relevant details.

Google may process information outside the United Kingdom, including in the United States. Google describes its transfer safeguards, including adequacy decisions, the UK Extension to the EU–US Data Privacy Framework and standard contractual clauses where applicable, in its data transfer information. Those arrangements depend on the service and transfer involved; contact us if you need further information about our use of Gmail.

We do not use support correspondence for marketing or automated decisions that have legal or similarly significant effects.

10. Visiting this website

Our support and privacy website is hosted by GitHub Pages. When you visit, GitHub receives technical information such as your IP address and information about your browser and request, which it uses to deliver and secure its services. This website does not include analytics, advertising, tracking pixels, external fonts or cookies set by us.

GitHub handles hosting information under its Privacy Statement, which explains its retention practices and international transfers. GitHub may process information outside the UK and describes safeguards including the UK Extension to the EU–US Data Privacy Framework and contractual safeguards where applicable. Visiting this website does not give us access to your calendars.

11. Your privacy rights

Depending on the circumstances and applicable law, you may be able to request access to, correction or deletion of personal information we hold, restrict its use, or obtain it in a portable form where that right applies. Contact us using the details below to make a request or raise a privacy concern.

You may object to our use of your personal information where we rely on legitimate interests.

We cannot remotely retrieve or delete calendar information stored on your Mac. Information you independently send us in correspondence can be addressed through a privacy request.

You can also complain to the UK Information Commissioner’s Office (ICO). Information about making a complaint and contacting the ICO is available at ico.org.uk/make-a-complaint.

12. Children and policy changes

Meteor is a general productivity application and is not specifically directed at children. It does not require an account or upload calendar information to the developer, regardless of the user’s age.

We may update this policy when Meteor’s features or our information-handling practices change. Any revised policy will show an updated date at the top.
